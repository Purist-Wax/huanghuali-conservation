Scientific Methodology

Our identification process follows established wood anatomical protocols. Every report is documented, reproducible, and defensible in legal proceedings.

1. Macroscopic Examination

Visual assessment of grain pattern, color, figure, and surface characteristics under controlled lighting conditions.

2. Microscopic Analysis

Examination of vessel elements, rays, and parenchyma. Note: Even major institutions such as Kew Gardens typically identify wood samples to genus level only. Our approach combines anatomical analysis with spectroscopic methods to achieve species-level identification within complex genera such as Dalbergia (200+ species) — a pragmatic, science-based methodology that provides actionable results for the art market and stands up to legal scrutiny.

3. Xylarium Comparison

Direct comparison with authenticated reference specimens from our documented collection.

4. Spectroscopic Analysis (when required)

For definitive species-level identification within complex genera, we employ spectroscopic methods to supplement anatomical analysis.

5. Documentation

Photographic records, measurement data, and written descriptions forming a complete analytical record.

6. Report Preparation

Written report with findings, confidence level, methodology, and photographic evidence. Reports specify the analytical methods employed and their limitations.

Documented Species

Our xylarium contains authenticated specimens of all major Chinese furniture woods. Each species entry includes macroscopic characteristics, microscopic features, and comparative analysis.

Dalbergia odorifera (with spectroscopic confirmation)

The most prized Chinese furniture wood. Golden-brown to reddish-brown heartwood with distinctive "ghost face" figuring. Characteristic sweet fragrance. Species-level identification requires spectroscopic analysis.

Pterocarpus santalinus

Deep purple-black heartwood that oxidizes to nearly black. Extremely dense and fine-textured. The imperial wood of the Qing dynasty. Anatomically distinct within genus.

Various Dalbergia spp.

Red hardwoods encompassing several Dalbergia species. Reddish-brown heartwood with variable figuring. Includes pre-CITES rosewoods. Species-level ID may require spectroscopy.
